Deoch 1 · 1999 · Age 9

IThe first computer

I got my first computer in 1999, when I was nine. It ran Windows 95, and it brought a run of firsts with it: my first email, my first website, my first Google account, and my first online RPG.

That game was LoD, Legend of Darkness, the Korean version of Dark Ages. What hooked me was that nobody advanced alone. Every class had its own role, and moving forward meant finding the people who filled the others. My friends and I spent every hour we could in it, right up until the free trial ran out.

Then my mom took my PC away, worried I'd become a game addict.

Too late. I'd already OD'd.

  • Aisling of Legend of Darkness, age 9 - Deoch 1
  • Ran the free trial to its last hour
The Deochs between

IINothing scratched the spot

Diablo II, MapleStory, Ragnarok, Aion and countless other RPGs and MMOs followed. None of them scratched the same spot.

Newer games let you advance on your own, which was almost unthinkable in DA. Dark Ages was built as a real role-playing game: you needed other people, and they needed you.

Those games gave me levels. None of them gave me a Legend.

  • Wandered other worlds, none of them home
The early Deochs · growing up in the game

IIICoins between keycaps

I had highs and lows growing up in that community. It taught me how to talk to strangers, and I grew socially both inside the game and out of it.

It's also where I wrote my first ever code: an AutoHotkey loop to level up my Wind Blade proficiency. Before that, I wedged coins between my keycaps to hold the keys down.

  • Learned to talk to strangers
  • First code: a Wind Blade loop in AutoHotkey

Deoch 222 to 224 · the last three months

VIIThe most giving people I've played with

That's been my journey for the last three months. So much help, so many shoutouts, so many sabonims and warm-hearted people. I have yet to be part of another online community with people this giving.

Sabonim: Korean for a respected teacher, the word I keep coming back to for the players who taught me.

DA went from a nostalgic memory to part of my daily login, and my Legend got longer than it ever did as a kid.

  • Taught by many sabonims - Deoch 222 to 224
Deoch 222 to 224 · still learning

VIIIStill learning the game

I'm still learning this game, and there's so much I haven't seen yet. Every run teaches me something.

The playbook

Start your own Legend

For anyone finding their way back. Everything that worked for me, in the order it happened. Each play is a mark waiting on your Legend.

  1. Look for the fire that's still burning

    Old games outlive their best years. Search YouTube and community servers for yours. Someone is probably still playing, and still talking about it.

    From chapter IV: one video, seventeen years later.

  2. Start somewhere humble, and be seen

    Farm the beginner spots. That's where veterans pass through, and where a new name gets noticed.

    From chapter IV: the crypt, where I met Zerio.

  3. Let a stranger teach you

    Ask the questions you think are obvious. Say yes when someone offers to walk you through. The guru was a stranger once, too.

    From chapter V.

  4. Put something in to belong

    Pick a guild and commit. Spend the gold, learn the rules, show up. Belonging is something you do, not something you wait for.

    From chapter V: my first reg, for The Balloon Boys.

  5. Show up for the big moments

    Community events are why these worlds still exist. Be in the room when a High Priestess is named. You'll remember it longer than any drop.

    From chapter VI: Yuka and Glioca.

  6. Chase the end game with people, while you can

    Gear is better shared. Small communities don't wait forever, so don't save the hard content for someday.

    From chapter VI: the AB99 greaves.

  7. Give it back

    Shout people out. Help the next new face in the crypt. Solve the riddle and share the answer. The community stays giving because people keep giving to it.

    From chapter VII: the sabonims.

  8. Stay a student

    However long your Legend gets, there's a quest that will take you 24 hours and someone who knows the shortcut. Ask, take notes, and stay humble about how much is left to learn.

    From chapter VIII: Demon Lord's Note.

  9. If you build things, build for the game

    The coins in the keycaps were a fix for a tedious grind. So was the AutoHotkey loop. So is Soondae. If something in the game is tedious for you, it's tedious for others too: make it easier and share it.

    From chapters III and IX.
